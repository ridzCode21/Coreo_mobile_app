Brand — the wave and the word

The mark is the wave: one continuous line, rising and falling, drawn at a 1:10 stroke ratio (12 stroke on a 120 grid) with round caps. It is the same wave the product uses to read the body, which makes the logo a promise, not a decoration. The wordmark is lowercase Poppins Light, tracked wide (+6px at 38px reference). Together they carry the line: a health app with a core. Clearspace equals one wave height on all sides. Below 90px of width the wave stands alone; it is also the app icon.

The geometry is law: path M 16 60 C 32 36, 48 36, 60 60 S 92 84, 104 60 on a 120 grid, stroke 12, round caps, no fill. Never: fill the wave, change the stroke ratio, add a second crest, put the orb inside the logotype, use gradients on the letterforms, or set the wordmark on mid-ramp atmosphere without a scrim. Ink on light stages, white on mid and night.

Interactive glass · highlights

The four laws of color. One: the atmosphere knows what time it is — morning screens live high in the ramp, evening screens deep, lock screens on night. Two: sky is neutral; color as meaning is reserved for status tints (amber attention, green on track), always paired with a word. Three: interactive surfaces are white glass. Four: Core Blue is the primary action — the one door on a screen, a circular button with a white arrow — and nothing else. Never on charts, tints, text or decoration. One per screen, maximum.

06

Voice — how the core speaks

Always You slept 7 hours 42. Deep sleep up 18 percent. Eat carbs around 4:30 and tonight's session will feel easy. Went over yesterday? Nothing turns red here. I adjust, we move on.

First person. Two to four sentences. Cites the user's own numbers. One next step. Never shames, never lectures, never pretends to know what it does not. Estimates are called estimates.

Never AMAZING! You CRUSHED your sleep goal! Time to SMASH that workout! As an AI language model, I cannot provide medical advice, however...

No exclamation marks, no emojis, no pep-talk clichés, no hedging boilerplate. And the hard lines: the core observes and suggests; it never diagnoses, never prescribes, and hands acute situations to real care immediately and clearly.

Locked vocabulary: "your core" · "Core Index" · "pillars" · "Smart Insight" · "the notebook" · "the check-in".

07

Usage — do and don't

Do

Map every screen's atmosphere to its time band. Morning high, evening deep, lock screens on night.

Do

Put every sub-18px text on a scrim or chip when it sits on atmosphere. The mid-ramp is the danger zone.

Do

Show honest states: calibrating, stale labeled stale, estimates named. Trust is the product.

Don't

Use dials, gauges or rings. Highs and lows are waves, always.

Don't

Use Core Blue anywhere except the single primary action. Never on charts, tints, text or decoration.

Don't

Introduce saturated accents, red error states, weights above 500, or a second typeface. If hierarchy is unclear, use white and scale.
