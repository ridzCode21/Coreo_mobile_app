02 · nav

Getting around · three takes on pillar nav

Once onboarding is done you live between four places: Today, Diet, Fitness, Wellness. The research is old and settled: visible bottom navigation beats hidden or gesture-only nav on both discoverability and speed, and hiding destinations cuts their usage roughly in half. So all three options keep the pillars visible and the core one touch away. 23a is the glass dock, a proven tab bar in Coreo's material with the orb raised in the center. 23b puts a pillar strip up top and lets you swipe between panes, keeping the talk bar at the bottom. 23c makes the orb itself the nav: tap and the pillars bloom around it. My call: 23a. Fastest to learn, always in thumb reach, and the raised orb keeps the brand at the center of every screen.

What users hate: AI guesses that can't be corrected, no favorites or repeat-meal logging, zero feedback on whether it's working, and core features behind paywalls. Fitness pillar · interview, home, quick loop

Researched Fitbod, Hevy, Strong, Nike Training Club. The praised onboarding asks the essentials: goal, place and equipment, current movement, the sports you play, injuries. 16a asks exactly those, in Coreo's voice. The loved features: plans that adapt to whatever equipment you have, and logging fast enough to use between sets. The hated ones: workouts that churn randomly so you can't see progress, apps blind to recovery and to everything you do outside the gym, cardio as an afterthought. So 16b, the pillar home, leads with a recovery-aware "today", and 16c is the quick loop: this week's sessions drawn from a movement library filtered to your goal and gear, stable enough to progress on.

Wellness pillar · interview, home, right now

Researched Calm, Headspace, Oura, Whoop. What wins: an app that soothes the moment you open it, guided breathing when anxiety is acute, and nudges that arrive before stress peaks, not after. What loses: huge content libraries with no "start here", the same pre-recorded session for everyone, and biometric dashboards that overwhelm people who don't want numbers. So 17a interviews for the one thing that weighs most, your real nights, and how you want Coreo to show up, including whether you want numbers at all. 17b is the pillar home: one wind-down, one reason why, no library. 17c is the screen that finds you mid-spike: a single breath, nothing to choose.
